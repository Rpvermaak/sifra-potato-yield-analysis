# Sifra Yield Analysis — Client Pitch Deck (10 Slides)

Use this as your presenter script for a 12–15 minute decision briefing + 5-minute demo.

---

## Slide 1 — Title & Business Question
**Title:** Why did Sifra fail in 2024 and recover in 2025?

**On-slide content (minimal):**
- Multi-site potato yield risk analysis (Soutpansberg)
- Objective: convert environmental data into season planning decisions
- Client decision today: approve a 90-day pilot

**Speaker notes:**
- Start with the paradox: same variety, opposite outcomes in consecutive seasons.
- Position the work as operational decision support, not academic research.
- Frame the meeting outcome: decide whether to run a focused pilot this season.

---

## Slide 2 — Executive Summary (30-second version)
**On-slide content:**
- North farm (rain shadow) consistently lower disease pressure
- South farm (mist belt) vulnerable during May–July humidity window
- 2025 pattern indicates recovery through better timing/management
- Recommendation: site-by-variety strategy + humidity-season protocol

**Speaker notes:**
- Keep this to 3–4 sentences only.
- Explain that the data supports a practical playbook, not just diagnostics.
- Promise specifics on interventions and pilot metrics in later slides.

---

## Slide 3 — Business Context & Why This Matters
**On-slide content:**
- Region has two microclimates despite geographic proximity
- Yield volatility creates avoidable margin risk
- Current planning is exposed to weather-driven disease windows

**Speaker notes:**
- Translate analytics into economics: unstable yields mean unstable cash flow and planning risk.
- Emphasize this is exactly where remote sensing can create early warning value.
- Keep this business-facing; avoid technical terms here.

---

## Slide 4 — Data & Method (One-Slide Credibility)
**On-slide content:**
- Sentinel-2 NDVI (crop vigor over time)
- NASA POWER weather (humidity, temperature, precipitation, solar)
- Soil context from SoilGrids
- Comparative analysis across years and locations

**Visual suggestion:**
- One simple pipeline graphic: Satellite + Weather + Soil → Risk Insights → Decisions

**Speaker notes:**
- Explain NDVI in one sentence: “a leading indicator of crop stress trajectory.”
- Stress reproducibility and low-cost monitoring using open data sources.
- Avoid modeling jargon; focus on trust and repeatability.

---

## Slide 5 — Finding 1: Location Is the Primary Driver
**On-slide content:**
- North (rain shadow) outperforms South (mist belt) across years
- Microclimate differences dominate baseline risk
- Site selection is the highest-leverage decision

**Visual suggestion:**
- North vs South comparative NDVI trend chart

**Speaker notes:**
- Lead with the operational implication: put Sifra where disease pressure is structurally lower.
- Clarify this is not a one-year anomaly; pattern is consistent.
- Keep one clear message: location first, then seasonal tactics.

---

## Slide 6 — Finding 2: 2024 Failure Pattern Matches Humidity-Season Risk
**On-slide content:**
- 2024 shows boom-then-collapse NDVI behavior
- Timing overlaps with June–July mist/humidity period
- Pattern is consistent with late blight pressure

**Visual suggestion:**
- NDVI curve with shaded May–July risk window and humidity overlay

**Speaker notes:**
- Do not over-claim causality; phrase as “strongly consistent with.”
- Explain why timing matters more than single weather events.
- Connect directly to preventive action timing.

---

## Slide 7 — Finding 3: 2025 Recovery Indicates Actionable Timing Improvement
**On-slide content:**
- 2025 NDVI trajectory is more stable/sustained
- Suggests improved timing and/or intervention effectiveness
- Recovery pattern can be operationalized as a playbook

**Visual suggestion:**
- Year-over-year NDVI comparison (2023 vs 2024 vs 2025)

**Speaker notes:**
- Position 2025 as proof that outcomes are manageable, not random.
- Emphasize repeatability: monitor early, intervene before decline phase.
- Keep confidence balanced: “promising evidence, now validate at pilot scale.”

---

## Slide 8 — Recommended Operating Model (What To Do Next Season)
**On-slide content:**
1. Prioritize Sifra in North farm blocks
2. Use disease-resilient varieties in South high-humidity blocks
3. Pre-emptive fungicide protocol focused on May–July
4. Weekly NDVI monitoring with threshold-based alerts

**Speaker notes:**
- Present this as a practical protocol, not a long transformation.
- Highlight that actions are familiar agronomy steps with better timing signals.
- Make clear this reduces downside risk while preserving upside potential.

---

## Slide 9 — 90-Day Pilot Proposal
**On-slide content:**
- Scope: 2–3 fields, North + South comparison
- Cadence: weekly monitoring, monthly decision review
- Deliverables: alert log, intervention timeline, field performance dashboard
- Success metrics:
  - earlier stress detection lead time
  - intervention timeliness
  - reduced decline severity during humidity season
  - yield/quality trend improvement versus baseline

**Speaker notes:**
- Keep ask concrete: small scope, clear metrics, clear governance.
- Position as low-regret experiment with immediate learning value.
- Ask for named point-of-contact and decision checkpoints.

---

## Slide 10 — Commercial Ask & Decision
**On-slide content:**
- Decision required: approve pilot now for current season window
- Two engagement options:
  - Insight Reporting (monitoring + monthly recommendations)
  - Decision Support (alerts + intervention playbooks + advisory)
- Next step: kickoff workshop and field selection

**Speaker notes:**
- End with one direct question: “Can we proceed with pilot kickoff this month?”
- Offer both options, but guide toward Decision Support if they want measurable impact.
- Confirm immediate next meeting date before ending.

---

## Live Demo Script (5 Minutes)
Use this directly after Slide 10.

1. Open the year-over-year NDVI chart and narrate the 2024 collapse vs 2025 stabilization.
2. Show North vs South comparison and state the site-allocation implication.
3. Overlay humidity period and point to risk-window alignment.
4. Show a mock “weekly alert” example and associated intervention action.
5. End on pilot dashboard mockup: what they will receive each month.

---

## Presenter Tips (High Impact)
- Keep each slide to one message and one decision implication.
- Use confidence-calibrated language: “evidence suggests,” “consistent with,” “validated in pilot.”
- Speak in margin/risk terms, not purely agronomic terms.
- Avoid notebook screens until demo section; deck first, technical proof second.
- Always convert charts into action: “therefore we should…”
